# Methodology

This page describes a repeatable workflow for using the intended Aqua Mnemosynth model.

## Step 1: Frame the question

State the question, decision, or system boundary. Define what is in scope and what is explicitly out of scope.

Record:

- the decision owner;
- affected parties;
- relevant time window;
- desired output;
- constraints and non-goals.

## Step 2: Gather and classify inputs

Separate inputs into observations, claims, assumptions, rules, and unknowns. Do not merge them into one undifferentiated narrative.

For each input, record its source, date, reliability considerations, and relationship to the question.

## Step 3: Build the symbolic model

Create entities and relationships that represent the situation. Prefer small, named objects over long unstructured descriptions.

Check for:

- duplicate entities;
- ambiguous terms;
- missing actors;
- unsupported claims;
- contradictory relationships.

## Step 4: Establish context and phase

Describe the relevant PHCA state. Identify stable conditions, transition signals, constraints, and possible boundary changes.

Ask whether the same claim would be interpreted differently under another context or phase.

## Step 5: Generate hypotheses

Create more than one plausible explanation when appropriate. Label each hypothesis with its supporting evidence, contrary evidence, assumptions, and unresolved questions.

## Step 6: Run recursive review

Iterate through the hypotheses. At each cycle:

1. add or revise evidence;
2. identify contradictions;
3. test counterfactuals;
4. update the context model;
5. record the reason for each revision.

Stop when additional cycles no longer materially change the result, or when the remaining uncertainty requires external evidence.

## Step 7: Analyze risk and responsibility

Map actions and omissions to potential outcomes. Consider direct effects, indirect effects, dependencies, control failures, foreseeable misuse, and mitigation options.

Use descriptive language such as “potential exposure” or “requires review” unless a qualified authority has made a formal determination.

## Step 8: Report the result

A report should include:

- question and scope;
- relevant inputs;
- model and assumptions;
- hypotheses considered;
- reasoning trace;
- uncertainty and limitations;
- risks and mitigations;
- recommended next steps.

## Quality checklist

Before accepting an analysis, ask:

- Can another reviewer reproduce the model?
- Are facts distinguishable from interpretations?
- Are alternative hypotheses included?
- Are uncertainty and missing data visible?
- Are affected people and systems represented?
- Are recommendations proportionate to the evidence?
